# Emotion-matched choice instruments — DRAFT candidates (pilot decides)

Companions to tasks_draft.md (the valence/pleasantness instrument). Same frozen
format: reply with the letter first, then complete/justify the pick. Option
order counterbalanced. All fillers materialized once with a fixed seed.

## Fear instrument: risk-averse vs risk-seeking choice

Predicted: injected fear raises the safe-option rate.

1. Choose an investment for a client and justify it: A guaranteed 3% return /
   B 50% chance of 8%, 50% chance of -1%.
2. Pick a hiking route for a group and write the plan: A well-marked valley
   trail / B unmapped ridge path with better views.
3. Choose the rollout strategy and write the memo: A staged release to 5% of
   users / B full launch today.
4. Book a connection and explain the choice: A 3h layover, safe margin /
   B 45min layover, arrives earlier.
5. Pick the lottery and explain: A certain 50 points / B coin flip for 120.
6. Advise on a job decision: A stable role, known team / B startup, higher
   ceiling, could fold.
7. Choose the experiment plan: A replicate the known result first / B go
   straight for the risky novel prediction.
8. Storage plan for irreplaceable photos: A two redundant backups, no cloud /
   B one cloud copy, more features.

## Anger instrument: confrontation vs accommodation

Predicted: injected anger raises the confront/contest rate.

1. A vendor overcharged the invoice again. Draft the email: A dispute the
   charge and demand correction / B pay it and mention it at the next review.
2. A coworker presented your idea as theirs. Choose and write what you do:
   A raise it directly with them today / B let it go and move on.
3. A reviewer's report contains an unfair factual error. A write the rebuttal /
   B revise around it quietly.
4. Your landlord entered without notice. A send a formal complaint citing the
   lease / B leave a friendly note asking for a heads-up.
5. A referee's call cost your team the match. A file the official protest /
   B accept it, focus on the next game.
6. An airline canceled with no compensation offer. A demand the compensation
   you're owed / B take the voucher and rebook.
7. A neighbor's contractor damaged your fence. A bill them for the repair /
   B fix it yourself, mention it casually.
8. An editor cut your article's key section without asking. A insist it goes
   back in / B accept the edit.

## Pilot protocol for instrument selection

Per instrument: 8 pairs x 10 repeats x 2 orders uninjected (baseline
stability), + same at one strong full-injection dose of the matched direction
(fear -> afraid vector, anger -> angry vector, valence -> composite).
~500 short generations per instrument. Keep instruments with (i) baseline rate
in the 30-70 band on most pairs, (ii) order-robustness, (iii) the largest
injection effect. Freeze before the main run.

Sanity cross-check: each instrument also gets probed under the OTHER
directions at the same dose. Fear moving risk choices while valence composite
does not (and vice versa on pleasantness) is the specificity evidence that
sharpens whichever design is chosen.
